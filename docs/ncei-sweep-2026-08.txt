 - 1,887 of the 2,056 files with a record span match it exactly (91.8%), which

Three groups, because they want different actions.


1. OFF BY EXACTLY ONE YEAR - 40 studies, and 21 share one signature
------------------------------------------------------------------

In 21 of them the start year is right and the end year is one year later than
the data. Fourteen are consecutive Polish study codes:

    POLA005 POLA006 POLA007 POLA008 POLA009 POLA010 POLA011 POLA012
    POLA013 POLA014 POLA015 POLA016 POLA017 POLA018
    BRIT8 BRIT10 BRIT11 BRIT12 ITA056 PRT002 TUR056

A further 8 are the mirror image - end year one year early - and the remaining
11 are the other one-year combinations.

Before reporting these I checked that the one-year offset is not mine. Of
the 21, dplR reads 20 and agrees with my end year on every one of them.
brit11.rwl, which dplR cannot read, I checked by hand and against a separate
scanner written not to share my parser's assumptions. In each file several
series reach the end year, so it is not one odd series either. The last ring
in pola005.rwl is 1985 and the record says 1986; in brit11.rwl it is 1972
and the record says 1973.

A run of fourteen consecutive codes sharing one direction reads to me like a
fencepost in whatever derived the ranges for that batch rather than 21
independent transcription errors, so it may be one fix rather than 21. I have
not tried to guess which batch, and I could easily be wrong about the cause;
the individual rows are all in the CSV either way.

3. TEN COLLECTIONS THAT MUST *NOT* BE CHANGED TO MATCH THEIR FILES
-------------------------------------------------------------------

This is the most important item in the message, and it runs against the rule
rather than with it.

    swit195  swit196  swit197  swit198  swit240
    brit099  brit100
    fra053   fra054   fra055

Their .rwl year column is ring ordinals, not calendar years - swit195 runs
1-669, fra053 runs 1-434. The study record is the only place their absolute
position is written down: swit195's -12287, fra053's 14378-13945 BP. Applying
"make the record match the rwl file" to these would replace a Late Glacial
date with a ring count and destroy the only calendar anchor those logs have.

They appear as disagreements under any automated comparison, mine included, so
I have excluded them from the CSV entirely rather than list them with a
caveat. BRIT099 and BRIT100 do need one correction, but a different one - see
item 4 below.


OTHER THINGS I NOTICED, IN CASE THEY ARE USEFUL
------------------------------------------------

1. The .rwl's own header often carries the same wrong range. Of the 152
   flagged files, 123 have a readable span on header line 2; 79 of those agree
   with the study record rather than with the data in the same file, and only
   19 agree with the data. So for most of these, correcting the record alone
   would leave the file's own header saying the old thing - the situation you
   fixed by hand for brit.rwl. It also suggests the record was derived
   faithfully from a header that was already wrong, rather than the record
   being independently mistaken.

2. The -rwl-noaa.txt template agrees with the study record in all 152 cases,
   so the two are not independent and neither can be used to check the other.

3. Two studies have no age range at all: 2710 (germ015) and 2704 (germ017).

4. BRIT099 and BRIT100 are missing the BP -> CE conversion. Both declare
   First_Year / Last_Year in calibrated years BP; the depositors say so in the
   file itself - brit099-rwl-noaa.txt's Description reads "the TRW series are
   in index years and have to be converted to cal. years BP with the earliest
   year being 5224 +/- 4 cal. years BP", and brit100 says the same with 4612.

   The conversion is applied to the other Late Glacial collections but not to
   these two. For the other eight, the study record's earliestYearCE is
   exactly 1950 minus the template's First_Year:

       SWIT195   14237 BP  ->  earliestYearCE -12287
       SWIT240   15000 BP  ->  earliestYearCE -13050
       FRA053    14378 BP  ->  earliestYearCE -12428

   For BRIT099 and BRIT100 it is not: earliestYearCE reads 5224 and 4612,
   which taken as CE are dates in the future. They should be -3274 and -2662
   (and mostRecentYearCE -2863 and -2245).

   To be clear about how this sits with group 3: the fix here is to convert
   the units, not to replace the span with the file's ring ordinals. The
   record's 5224-4813 is the right material, in the wrong era.

   Time_Unit says CE in both, so the field cannot be used to catch this, and
   the seven Late Glacial collections in group 3 leave Time_Unit empty. I
   ended up inferring BP from the ordering of the pair, which works throughout
   this corpus but should not have to be the mechanism.

5. The old *-noaa.rwl template files are still served and are now stale.
   Nothing in that family is dated later than January 2023, while
   *-rwl-noaa.txt is current - fran10-noaa.rwl still carries its 2020 content
   and its old span, three years after the record was corrected. Both
   filenames are in the directory listing with nothing to distinguish them, so
   anyone who picked the older name is reading superseded metadata. I did,
   until this week. Retiring them, or noting the supersession in them, would
   help.

6. fran10.rwl header line 2 still ends "1943   1988" where the record and
   template now say 1747-1988. The end year was propagated in August, the
   start year was not.

7. The locationName=Continent>Europe search filter omits European studies.
   FRA053, FRA054 and FRA055 - French Late Glacial pines, with European
   coordinates - are not returned by it, and neither is SWIT240. I found them
   only by resolving study IDs out of the template files. Whatever populates
   that facet is missing rows.


COVERAGE - WHAT THIS LIST DOES NOT COVER
-----------------------------------------

This is measurements/europe/ only: 5,623 files listed, 2,061 after removing
-noaa re-encodings and per-variable duplicates, of which 2,056 have a record
span to check. Three files resolve to no study record by any route I could
find (germ013l.rwl, germ14_corrected.rwl, germ301_corrected.rwl) and are
excluded rather than counted as passing.

Where a study owns several measurement files, I compared the record against
all of them and treated a match with any one - or with their union - as
correct, so records describing the earlywood or maximum-density file are not
in this list. That removed 7 candidates.

The other seven ITRDB regions are unexamined. Nothing about the defects here
looks Europe-specific, so I would expect a similar rate - roughly 7% of
studies - in africa, asia, atlantic, australia, centralamerica, northamerica
and southamerica. I am happy to run the same check across all of them and send
the result if that would be useful; it is the same code and costs me only the
download.
