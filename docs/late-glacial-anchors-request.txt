Dear Dr. Reinig, dear Prof. Büntgen,

I build an interactive view of the ITRDB's European measurement files: every
individual log drawn as one bar on a shared calendar axis, 73,390 of them so
far, at https://treerings.howardalandman.org. I am writing because the oldest
material in the archive is also the material I cannot place, and your IntCal20
work on the Swiss Late Glacial pines is, as far as I can tell, where the
answer lives.

The five Swiss files number their rings from an arbitrary origin and declare
only a site-wide range, so nothing in the archive says where each one sits:

  File     Logs  Ordinals   Declared (cal BP)   Example series IDs
  SWIT195     5  1-669      14237-12750         DAECHAL1, KFKA2090
  SWIT196     7  1-375      14237-12750         DAEALCH2, KFKA2640
  SWIT197     9  1-318      14237-12750         DAEALCH3, KFKA3000
  SWIT198    20  1-286      14237-12750         DAEBOECH, KFKA1100
  SWIT240    95  53-1434    15000-14000         GAE0010, LAN0650

All four Daettnau files share the same declared range, and NCEI's
correlation-stats output for SWIT196 gives "Beginning year: 1". Until I have
a position for each file, these 136 logs stay off the calendar axis rather
than being drawn at a date nobody published.

My questions:

 1. Is there a published calendar position for each of these five files - the
    cal BP of the file's own ring 1, with its uncertainty - following the
    IntCal20 placement, or your 2025 Tree-Ring Research paper on dating
    during the Younger Dryas, which I have not yet been able to read? A
    pointer to a table or dataset would be ideal.

 2. Does the internal alignment of the ITRDB files still hold, or were any
    series re-crossdated or re-assigned in later work, so that a single offset
    per file would be wrong?

 3. SWIT240's measured span is exactly 1,382 rings. Is it the floating
    1382-ring chronology of Kromer et al. (2004, Radiocarbon 46(3)), and if
    so, is that Cariaco-based anchor superseded by IntCal20?

One question on the Fenland yews (BRIT099, BRIT100), which I have placed.
The templates say the earliest year is 5224 +/-4 and 4612 +/-6 cal BP.
Pinning that year to the first ring of each file leaves the declared end year
off by 1 and 35 years. Pinning it to the first year covered by at least two
series (ordinals 841 and 635) makes both declared ends match exactly in both
files, so that is the reading I used:

  BRIT099  ordinal 841 = 5224 cal BP  ->  ordinal 1252 = 4813 cal BP
  BRIT100  ordinal 635 = 4612 cal BP  ->  ordinal 1052 = 4195 cal BP

 4. Is that the intended meaning of "earliest year"? The 1 and 5 unreplicated
    leading rings then sit before the stated date.

Separately, NCEI's study records give earliestYearCE as 5224 and 4612 for
these two - the BP values unconverted, so dates in the future. I have
reported that to NCEI; it needs nothing from you.

The pipeline is open source at https://github.com/HowardALandman/TreeRings
if you want to check how the files are read. I would be glad to credit
whatever you can share on the site.
