Subject: Hohenheim ring-width measurements - a public per-log view

Dear Dr. Westphal,

I build an interactive view of tree-ring measurement data: every individual
log drawn as one bar on a shared calendar axis, coloured by taxon, with its
ring-width curve a click away. It currently shows 73,390 logs from 2,038
European sites in the International Tree-Ring Data Bank, at

  https://treerings.howardalandman.org

The project began with the Hohenheim chronology in mind, and I understand
that the collection and its measurement records are now with CEZA, after the
Hohenheim working group closed in February. I am writing to ask whether, and
on what terms, CEZA might share the ring-width measurements.

The view has a long hole that I think the Hohenheim material would fill. In
the public archive no log is alive between 11746 BC and 5408 BC; before that
there are only three anchored French Late Glacial pine collections, and after
it the record is continuous. But I would be glad of whatever material CEZA is
able to release, from any period, not only the part that fills the gap.

What would be useful, in any format you already have (Tucson .rwl,
Heidelberg .fh, TRiDaS, or a database export):

  - per-series ring widths, or at least each series' first and last dated
    year;
  - the taxon and the site or find location of each series;
  - whether each series is calendar-dated or floating, and on what basis.

Some questions:

 1. Is any part of the measurement collection cleared for release, or is it
    planned to be, for example once your database is complete?

 2. Under what conditions could it be used? I would credit CEZA and the
    Hohenheim laboratory on the site and in any publication, and can keep the
    data itself off public download if needed and show only the drawn bars.

 3. If a public release is planned (ITRDB, PANGAEA, Zenodo or similar), is
    there a rough timetable, so that I can wait for it rather than ask you to
    send anything twice?

The pipeline is open source at https://github.com/HowardALandman/TreeRings
if you want to see how files are read and checked. Its parser agrees with
dplR on the dated span of every series that dplR can read. I am also happy
to write in German if that is easier.

With thanks and best regards,

Howard Landman
